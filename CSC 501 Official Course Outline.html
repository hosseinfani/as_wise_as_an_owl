Course Overview

Every piece of data begins with a real-world entity, be it a person, object, event, or phenomenon. This graduate course follows the data odyssey from reality to machine-understandable yet human-interpretable representation. We begin by (1) modeling real-world entities conceptually, capturing their essential properties and relationships; then (2) transforming these conceptual models into computable representations using structured data models such as the relational model, as well as semi-structured representations such as XML and JSON. Finally, (3) we move beyond to Graph Data Models including Knowledge Graphs and use Semantic Web technologies including RDF, RDFS, and OWL, to give data explicit meaning (semantics) and enable machines (e.g., large language models or agents) to reason and ground their actions or responses over structured and semantically rich knowledge via Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Along the way, students develop an understanding of how the choice of data model shapes what can be represented, queried, discovered, and ultimately understood by both humans and machines.

Course Objectives and Learning Outcomes

Successful students in this course will have demonstrated proficiency in the following competencies:

  • Data Modelling: Choose an appropriate data model for a given application and design an effective schema within it
  • Cost Analysis: Identify an appropriate cost model for a given application and design a schema and/or algorithm to minimize cost
  • Data Analytics: Illuminate insights effectively in various forms of unsanitized data through extract-transform-load (ETL) processes and well-written queries
  • Communicating Design Decisions: Express strong arguments to justify your design decisions in data science pipelines in both written and oral form
  • Project Planning: Create feasible work plans for a group project and adjust future planning based on current velocity
  • Personal Effectiveness: Self-assess challenges that you are facing and plan how to mitigate them

Topics

The course will cover data models for representing and manipulating data at different abstraction levels, including:

  1. Conceptual Representation: Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD)
  2. Logical Representation:
    • Structured: Relational
    • Semi-structured: JSON, XML 
  3. Semantic Representation: Graph Data Models, Knowledge Graphs
  4. Algorithms and Applications: Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Social Search and Recommendation, Information Diffusion, Influence Analysis

Assessment

A semester-long group project, a midterm exam, and a final exam are the main components of the assessment, as well as additional bonus points. The details are:

  • This course is project-driven. Students work in groups to complete a data science project over one semester, following six project milestones. The project focuses on applying the theories and technologies introduced in the course to address a real-world knowledge representation problem using appropriate data models. Each group defines a clear problem statement, creates or sources a dataset, and designs a data model to support their analysis. At each milestone, students submit a project deliverable, including a report prepared according to the guidelines provided in the project manual.
  • Unless an alternative consideration is approved in accordance with the Academic Concessions Regulation, a mark of zero will be assigned for a missed assessment item or late submission. There are no make-up examinations. If a midterm examination is missed, its weight will be transferred to the final examination. If the final examination is missed, the student’s overall collected mark will be assigned as the final course grade.

Student Experience of Learning (SEL)

I value your feedback on this course. Towards the end of term you will have the opportunity to complete a confidential Student Experience of Learning (SEL) survey regarding your learning experience. The survey is vital to providing feedback to me regarding the course and my teaching, as well as to help the department improve the overall program for students in the future. When it is time for you to complete the survey, you will receive an email inviting you to do so. If you do not receive an email invitation, you can go directly to the SEL site
